# REFERENCE_STYLE_DNA

## Source and inspection

Primary reference: [Hera, Your AI Motion Designer (Demo)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lwnxr6VW4B8). I inspected the actual 30-second YouTube player, sampling the full clip at roughly three-second intervals and the 10–15 second list reveal at roughly one-second intervals. I also inspected the storyboard sheets served by that YouTube page; their low-resolution tiles confirm the sequence between player seeks. The video page has no description, and YouTube's transcript export reports that no transcript is available.

The player exposed video frames and duration, but not a downloadable audio asset or waveform. I therefore make no claim about its BPM, exact music accents, or sound-effect sync. The style proof is silent and tests the visual language only.

## A. Visual identity

The reference is a 2D motion-design demo of a generative interface. It keeps one persistent, pale peach/pink canvas with a fine square grid and softly blurred warm blooms. White UI surfaces float on that canvas with slight shadows. Near-black sans-serif text and a coral submit button provide nearly all the contrast. There is no live footage, realistic 3D set, or full-page product screen.

The distinctive object sequence is small and centered: a compact prompt field; a modest white list card above it; a narrow phone-shaped mock-up; then a dense gallery. The background remains recognizable through the changes.

## B. Motion identity

The prompt copy types on inside a long white capsule. A black attachment circle and coral circular send control stay anchored at its right edge. On the list reveal around 10.8–12s, the answer card appears above the composer; the composer drops toward the lower part of the frame. Individual white rows arrive slightly askew, then settle into a straight stack. The scene at 11s shows the rows still tilted; by 12s the four rows are aligned.

Around 15–18s a narrow phone panel rises and expands its content in place. A black pointer enters around 18s and moves over the panel. The main motion comes from element position, scale, opacity and a little rotation; it is deliberately legible rather than continuously busy.

## C. Composition identity

The canvas is mostly negative space. Until the gallery reveal, one primary object occupies the center at a time. When a result card is generated, it sits above the composer instead of replacing the full frame; both remain visible as a cause-and-result pair. The list card is about half the frame width and under half its height. The phone mock-up is narrow and tall. The gallery at 22–26s is the one deliberate high-density frame.

Typography is compact interface copy with a clear weight jump for generated headings. The opening and closing brand lockups are larger, centered, and isolated. Marketing copy is minimal.

## D. Editing identity

The edit is a sequence of prompt → generated object → next prompt, all on the same canvas. The list reveal is quick: a typed request, the composer shifts, skewed rows appear, then the rows settle. A new request clears the list before the phone example begins. Near 22s the camera pulls out quickly from the phone UI to a tiled gallery of prior outputs; it then resolves to a centered Hera end card at 27s. There are no conventional problem/solution scenes or screenshot montages.

## E. Audio identity

The YouTube page reports 0:30 and exposes no transcript. This inspection surface did not provide the reference audio stream or waveform for analysis. Audio rhythm, instrumentation, and individual impact timing remain unverified. No music or sound-effect timing is invented for the visual proof.

## F. Production approach

The observed piece can be reproduced with a 2D vector/DOM composition and a seekable GSAP timeline: a reusable gradient/grid plate, compact floating surfaces, staggered element transforms, a pointer, and a final scale pullback into an authored gallery. The proof uses that visual approach with a source-based reconstruction of Smile's actual HR portal screens. Hera's prompt composer belongs to Hera's product; it is not represented as a Smile feature. The proof does not use the Hera logo, prompt wording, or crypto artwork. More elaborate 3D software is not needed for the observed motion.

## Product accuracy guardrails

The Smile proof follows the actual resume-screening route: HR selects a published role, adds resume files from a computer or connected cloud storage, submits them for screening, opens the Applicants list, views an applicant, reviews the saved CV analysis and role criteria, then makes the HR decision. The portal also accepts candidate applications through public or private role links and offers a single-resume form. These are different entry paths; the proof shows the HR bulk-screening route only. Interview invitations, voice or Live Avatar interviews, interview review, and face-to-face scheduling belong to later stages and are not shown in this seven-second proof.

All candidate and role values in the reconstruction are demo data. The score, recommendation, summary, strengths, and gaps are illustrative examples, not a real applicant result or a promised output. The interface keeps an `ILLUSTRATIVE UI · DEMO DATA · TIMING CONDENSED` mark visible so the short style test is not mistaken for live processing time.
